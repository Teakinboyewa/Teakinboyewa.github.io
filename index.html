About

I am a geographer interested in how generative AI can support spatial analysis and geographic inquiry, with applications in population health and natural hazards. My research spans GeoAI, autonomous GIS, human mobility, health geography, and disaster assessment. I am currently a Ph.D. student in the Department of Geography at The Pennsylvania State University, where I work as a Graduate Research and Teaching Assistant, and I expect to graduate in Spring 2027.

A major part of my research asks whether AI can take part in geographic inquiry rather than simply carry out GIS tasks. Sound spatial analysis depends on careful choices about data, scale, spatial dependence, and context, and the expertise this demands keeps it out of reach for many who need it. I have explored this question through GIS Copilot, which lets people run spatial analyses in natural language within an established GIS platform, and GIS Co-Scientist, a human-AI framework that carries out inquiries from research question to draft manuscript, with human checkpoints where geographic judgment most affects inference. Independent expert review of GIS Co-Scientist revealed a key insight: a workflow can run successfully and still be geographically wrong.

Much of my applied research uses large-scale smartphone-based visitation data to understand how people interact with places in their everyday lives, which conventional neighborhood measures based on where people live do not capture. I have used these data to examine coronary heart disease across U.S. neighborhoods, to estimate tract-level health measures, and to study how food visits changed during the COVID-19 pandemic across communities with varying income levels and racial composition. In natural hazards, I have used a large multimodal model to estimate floodwater depth from flood photographs without task-specific training.

Looking forward, I am particularly interested in trustworthy GeoAI: methods that account for scale, spatial dependence, and context, check claims against evidence, quantify uncertainty, and recognize when human judgment is needed. I also plan to study how people's daily activities shape their environmental exposure and health, and how photos, videos, and posts shared by residents can support disaster response and recovery. I welcome opportunities for collaboration and conversation around these topics.

My background is in surveying and remote sensing. I earned a B.S. in Surveying and Geoinformatics and an M.S. in Remote Sensing and GIS from the Federal University of Technology, Akure, Nigeria, and worked as a field surveyor and GIS analyst before coming to Penn State. In 2026 I was an AI Research Intern at bAI Labs.
