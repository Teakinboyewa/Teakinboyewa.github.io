Temitope Akinboyewa is a PhD candidate in Geography at The Pennsylvania State University, where he works with Dr. Zhenlong Li in the Department of Geography within the College of Earth and Mineral Sciences. His research sits at the intersection of geographic information science, artificial intelligence, and spatial analysis, with a focus on building autonomous GIS—systems in which large language models and multimodal foundation models can plan, retrieve, and execute geospatial analyses with minimal human steering.

His recent work has introduced an autonomous GIS agent framework for geospatial data retrieval, the GIS Copilot system for LLM-driven spatial analysis, a multi-agent AI co-scientist framework for geospatial scientific inquiry, and a framework for Geospatial Agentic Services built on interoperable standards.

Alongside this methodological work, he applies large multimodal models to disaster response—in particular, rapid flood mapping from imagery—and uses socially-sensed mobility data to study the geographic determinants of population health at the neighborhood scale. His work has appeared in International Journal of Digital Earth, PLOS One, and Computational Urban Science.

He passed his Doctoral Qualifying Examination in April 2024 and his Comprehensive Examination in March 2025. Before joining Penn State, he earned a Bachelor of Technology from the Federal University of Technology, Akure.
