What are GIS and remote sensing?

The two big ideas behind this course, in plain words.

Time: about 15 minutesLevel: complete beginner

Watch

“What is GIS? Geographic Information Systems Explained in 1 Minute” by Felt. Watch on YouTube.

More videos:

  • What is a GIS (Esri Ireland)
  • NASA ARSET: Introduction to Satellite Remote Sensing (Part 1/5) (NASA Video)

In plain words

Remote sensing means learning about a place without going there. A camera-like sensor on a satellite, plane, or drone measures the light coming off the ground. Because forests, water, soil, and cities reflect light differently, we can tell them apart in the pictures, even in kinds of light our eyes cannot see.

GIS (geographic information system) is software for working with anything that has a location. It lets you put many layers of information on top of each other, such as a satellite image, roads, rivers, and town names, and then measure, compare, and map them.

Put simply: remote sensing collects the pictures, and GIS helps you analyze them and make maps. In this course you will use both.

A simple example

Imagine you want to know how much forest was cut near a new road. Remote sensing gives you satellite images from before and after the road was built. GIS lets you draw a zone 1 km around the road, compare the two images inside that zone, and calculate how many hectares of forest were lost.

Words you will hear a lot

Satellite image
A picture of the Earth taken from space, made of tiny squares called pixels.
Pixel
One small square in an image. Each pixel stores a number.
Layer
One set of information on a map, such as roads, or an image.
Map
A picture that shows where things are, with a legend, a scale, and a title.
